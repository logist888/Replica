An open English test

Find out your English level in half an hour

A free test with no sign-up. Three short parts — words, reading and listening — and at the end your level on the A1 to C2 scale.

How the test works

Three parts, one after another. Find a quiet spot, and have headphones ready for the last part.

  1. 01

    Words about 4 minutes

    You see 60 words, one at a time. Answer “yes” if you know what a word means, and “no” if you don’t or aren’t sure. Some of the words are made up, so we can tell real knowledge from a habit of clicking “yes”.

  2. 02

    Reading about 15 minutes

    Ten short texts, from notices to articles, with three questions each. The texts are chosen to match your level from the first part, so you are not stuck with ones that are too easy or too hard.

  3. 03

    Listening about 15 minutes

    Four recordings, from everyday conversations to talks on harder topics. You can play each one once or twice and answer while you listen.

  4. 04

    Your result right after the test

    You see your level range, for example B1–B2, and get a link to a page with your result. You can save it.

What the levels mean

The CEFR scale is used all over the world. Here is what each level means in short.

  • A1

    Beginner

    You understand and use simple phrases about yourself and everyday things.

  • A2

    Elementary

    You manage everyday situations: shopping, travel, meeting people.

  • B1

    Intermediate

    You follow the main points of clear speech and can talk about your experience and plans.

  • B2

    Upper intermediate

    You chat easily about familiar topics, read articles and watch films without subtitles.

  • C1

    Advanced

    You speak and write flexibly and precisely, and understand long, demanding texts.

  • C2

    Proficient

    You use the language almost as freely as an educated native speaker.

Why the test is open

Most English level tests are closed: you don’t know how they were made or how far to trust them. We do the opposite.

  1. 01

    You can check everything

    The scoring method, the rules and the code are public. Anyone can see how your result is worked out.

  2. 02

    You can use everything

    Texts and questions are released under CC BY-SA 4.0 and the code under the MIT licence. Teachers and schools can put them to work.

  3. 03

    We are honest about accuracy

    We don’t promise what we haven’t verified. For now the test shows a range, not a single letter, and we say so plainly.

How we work out your level

Every question has a “weight”: how hard it is. The more hard questions you get right, the higher your level. This is a standard approach in testing, known as the Rasch model.

A question’s weight can only be learned from how real people answer it. That is why we are running a pilot: we need about 300 people to settle the weight of each question. Until then the weights are rough, which is why we show a range.

What we keep about you

We don’t ask for your name, email or phone number, and we don’t store your IP address. We keep your answers and whatever you choose to add: native language, years of study, the result of another test. It is used only to improve the questions.

Your result opens through a long random link. Anyone who has the link can see the result, so don’t post it if you don’t want that.

Questions and answers

Is this an official certificate?

No. The result does not replace an exam for a university or an employer. The test is there to help you see where you stand and what to learn next.

Can I trust the result?

With a caveat, for now. The questions are still being tested on people, so we show a range and call the result provisional. Accuracy will improve after the pilot.

Do I need to register?

No. Open the page, tick two boxes and start.

How long does it take?

Usually 30 to 40 minutes. Please don’t close the tab: progress is not saved, and you would have to start again.

Do I need headphones?

For the third part, yes, or speakers and a quiet room.

Can I take the test again?

You can, but the test works best the first time, when you haven’t seen the questions. We mark repeat attempts in the data.

Who is the test for?

Adults and teenagers from 16 who are learning English. The test and all its questions are in English.

How do I delete my data?

Open an issue in the project’s GitHub repository and include your session ID: the last characters of your result link.

Help us check the test

We need about 300 people to refine the questions. You spend half an hour and get your level, and the open test becomes more accurate.
